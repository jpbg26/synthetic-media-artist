The proposition

Meaningful human creative direction—not the mere use or avoidance of artificial intelligence—is central to artistic authorship.

01 / MANIFESTO

The proposed definition

A Synthetic Media Artist (SMA) is an individual who uses generative artificial intelligence extensively within an artistic or creative workflow while maintaining substantial human direction, intentionality, and authorship. Through prompting, iteration, prototyping, performance, composition, editing, and other techniques, the artist shapes the work in pursuit of a distinct artistic vision.

02 / HUMAN AUTHORSHIP

What makes artistic direction substantial?

Substantiality is measured by the significance of human artistic decisions, not prompt length or hours spent. The manifesto examines five dimensions: intentional direction, developed artistic capacity, creative agency, demonstrable authorship, and creative investment. A carefully directed five-minute creation may express more authorship than hours of undirected generation.

03 / HISTORICAL FOUNDATIONS

Authorship beyond execution

Sol LeWitt's instruction-based works, Harold Cohen's AARON, Vera Molnár's algorithmic art, John Cage's chance operations, and Brian Eno's generative music offer precedents for creative direction through instructions, systems, and discovery. Studio fabrication, collage, and photography further demonstrate that artistic authorship need not mean manually executing every component.

04 / THE MEDIUM

Art through technological mediation

Every medium has distinct constraints and possibilities. Generative AI transforms learned relationships into new outputs, while the artist shapes the work through inputs, evaluation, selection, and further editing. Two routes to authorship are intentional realization of a vision and intentional discovery through guided experimentation.

05 / PRACTICE

Slop, craft, slopcore, and SMA

Casual generation and applying a ready-made prompt to a selfie can be legitimate forms of template-based generative craft without necessarily constituting SMA. AI slop is a contested label; slopcore is an intentional aesthetic; SMA is defined by substantial human creative direction. These distinctions concern creative practice, not the inherent worth of a result.

06 / CRITIQUES & RESPONSES

Commissioner or artist? The black-box objection

Critics compare AI prompting to commissioning an illustrator, or argue that a neural network's opaque process makes artistic control illusory. The manifesto distinguishes passive requests from consequential creative direction, and explains how learned micro-adjustments, selection, iteration, and editing in other applications give the SMA practical control without requiring perfect predictability.

Example: Comic creation

An SMA develops a storyboard, character backgrounds, dialogue, and visual style before generating panels, then revises continuity, layout, and expressions. A minimally directed workflow may involve a two-line request and acceptance of the first comic.

08 / MANIFESTO

Ethics and artistic responsibility

Human artists learn from predecessors, but machine training and human learning are not identical. Training-data consent, copyright, and compensation remain important questions. Generative systems can reproduce protected expression even without a deliberate copying request. Artistic merit, training legality, and output infringement must be evaluated separately.

09 / AESTHETICS

Practice and possibilities

The accompanying paper develops eight principles of artistic practice, including creative conception, human direction, intentional revision, and transparency. A single discussion of creative labor explains the artist’s responsibility to provide a brief statement of purpose, rationale, and workflow. It also explores the medium’s aesthetic possibilities and its potential to widen access to creation. Participatory Generativity is a distinctive optional capability: an artist can create an interactive framework that adapts to audience choices without making interactivity a requirement of SMA.

10 / MANIFESTO

About this proposal

Proposed by Justin Plevinski. Version 1.0, October 14, 2026. This introductory public edition develops a cohesive framework for understanding and defending Synthetic Media Art as an art form. It acknowledges earlier practice associated with Bill Posters and documented use of “synthetic media art” by Annka Kultys Gallery in 2021, without claiming to identify the term’s originator. Plevinski developed the initial formulation independently and now situates it within prior scholarship, including Epstein and colleagues’ work on generative AI as an artistic medium and Memo Akten’s research on meaningful human control. The contribution proposed here is a particular synthesis and five-dimensional account of substantial human direction, rather than priority over the terminology or the broader ideas. The full academic argument is available on the Academic Argument page; its references are provided for further examination.
