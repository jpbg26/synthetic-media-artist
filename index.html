Synthetic
Media Artist.

Art is not defined by the tool. It is shaped by the intention, judgment, and creative agency of the person using it.

Justin Plevinski · Version 1.0 · October 14, 2026
The proposition

Meaningful human creative direction—not the mere use or avoidance of artificial intelligence—is central to artistic authorship.

01 / MANIFESTO

The proposed definition

A Synthetic Media Artist (SMA) is an individual who uses generative artificial intelligence extensively within an artistic or creative workflow while maintaining substantial human direction, intentionality, and authorship. Through prompting, iteration, prototyping, performance, composition, editing, and other techniques, the artist shapes the work in pursuit of a distinct artistic vision.

02 / MANIFESTO

Authorship beyond execution

Impressionism, Duchamp's Fountain, and conceptual art demonstrate how artistic practices have challenged expectations about technique and authorship. They do not prove all AI content is art, but they challenge the idea that manual fabrication is the sole test of artistic legitimacy. Like a film director or music producer, an SMA may direct a complex creative process without personally executing every technical component.

03 / MANIFESTO

AI slop, slopcore, and SMA

AI slop is often a critical label for mass-produced, low-effort generative content. Slopcore can be a deliberate aesthetic embracing the surreal, artificial, or absurd. SMA instead describes a practice involving substantial human creative direction. These categories are not mutually exclusive: an SMA can intentionally produce slopcore.

04 / MANIFESTO

Example: Comic creation

An SMA develops a storyboard, character backgrounds, dialogue, and visual style before generating panels, then revises continuity, layout, and expressions. A minimally directed workflow may involve a two-line request and acceptance of the first comic.

05 / MANIFESTO

Example: Multi-agent songwriting

An SMA designs specialized creative roles for songwriting, critiques and rewrites lyrics, refines musical style, and may record a personal performance into Suno. A minimally directed workflow asks for a song on a topic and accepts the result.

06 / MANIFESTO

Example: Hybrid music production

An SMA creates a backing track in Suno, imports it into Logic Pro, records vocals, adds layers, restructures the arrangement, and mixes the finished song.

07 / MANIFESTO

Example: Photoshop composition

An SMA generates multiple separate visual assets, imports them into Photoshop, and uses layers, masks, perspective adjustments, lighting, and color grading to assemble an original composition. The generative outputs are materials, not the finished artwork.

08 / MANIFESTO

Ethics and artistic responsibility

Human artists learn from predecessors, but machine training and human learning are not identical. Training-data consent, copyright, and compensation remain important questions. Generative systems can reproduce protected expression even without a deliberate copying request. Artistic merit, training legality, and output infringement must be evaluated separately.
