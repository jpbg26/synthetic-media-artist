A framework for human creativity with generative AI

Synthetic Media
Art and Creation

Images, songs, and music videos can reflect different degrees of human direction. Synthetic Media Creations recognize substantial creative input; Synthetic Media Art involves more rigorous artistic direction and development.

Justin Plevinski · Version 1.1 · Originally published October 14, 2026
Last updated: October 21, 2026
The proposition

Human intention, judgment, and creative agency shape synthetic media. The depth and rigor of that direction distinguish creation from the artistic practice proposed here.

01 / MANIFESTO

Art and creation: the proposed framework

AI Slop refers here to generative content produced with minimal or uncritical human direction, where the system supplies the substantive creative decisions and the user accepts them without purposeful evaluation. A generic request followed by acceptance of the first output may fit this category. The distinction concerns the creator’s role: visual polish, prompt length, and production time alone do not determine whether something is slop, SMC, or SMA.

Synthetic Media Creations (SMC) are images, songs, music videos, and other works made with substantial human input and direction while leaving more expressive decisions to the generative system. Original lyrics turned into a song through a couple of Suno iterations, or a developed paragraph used as an image prompt with limited revision, would ordinarily fit here.

Synthetic Media Art (SMA) involves more rigorously developed human artistic direction. A Synthetic Media Artist shapes a distinct vision through consequential decisions in conception, prompting, references, evaluation, iteration, performance, composition, or editing. Purposefully refining lyrics and musical style across many versions, or developing detailed visual directions with creator-drawn references and targeted revisions, provides stronger evidence of SMA.

AI slop, SMC, and SMA describe increasing degrees and rigor of human direction. These categories describe a workflow and the creator’s role; the same person can produce work in all three categories. They do not rank a finished work’s beauty, usefulness, or emotional impact. Prompt length and iteration counts help illustrate the distinction, but the significance of the human decisions determines the assessment.

02 / HUMAN AUTHORSHIP

What makes artistic direction substantial?

The framework examines five dimensions: intentional direction, developed artistic capacity, creative agency, demonstrable authorship, and creative investment. SMC includes substantial human input with broader delegation to the model; SMA requires more rigorous direction of the resulting expression. A carefully directed five-minute work may demonstrate more artistic authorship than hours of undirected generation.

03 / HISTORICAL FOUNDATIONS

Authorship beyond execution

Sol LeWitt's instruction-based works, Harold Cohen's AARON, Vera Molnár's algorithmic art, John Cage's chance operations, and Brian Eno's generative music offer precedents for creative direction through instructions, systems, and discovery. Studio fabrication, collage, and photography further demonstrate that artistic authorship need not mean manually executing every component.

04 / THE MEDIUM

Art through technological mediation

Every medium has distinct constraints and possibilities. Generative AI transforms learned relationships into new outputs, while the artist shapes the work through inputs, evaluation, selection, and further editing. Two routes to authorship are intentional realization of a vision and intentional discovery through guided experimentation.

05 / PRACTICE

Slop, SMC, and SMA

Synthetic Media Creations (SMC) occupy a step between minimally directed generation and Synthetic Media Art: substantial human input and direction, with more expressive decisions delegated to the model. Writing lyrics and selecting a Suno recording after a couple of iterations would ordinarily be SMC; developing and refining the lyrics and musical style through purposeful iteration provides stronger evidence of SMA. Prompt length and iteration counts are indicators, not fixed thresholds. Template-based generative craft and the slopcore aesthetic can overlap these practices.

06 / CRITIQUES & RESPONSES

Commissioner or artist? The black-box objection

Critics compare AI prompting to commissioning an illustrator, or argue that a neural network's opaque process makes artistic control illusory. The manifesto distinguishes passive requests from consequential creative direction, and explains how learned micro-adjustments, selection, iteration, and editing in other applications give the SMA practical control without requiring perfect predictability.

Example: Comic creation

A creator who supplies a developed premise, some dialogue, and a style while delegating most panel design may produce SMC. An SMA develops a storyboard, character backgrounds, dialogue, and visual style, then purposefully refines continuity, layout, and expressions.

08 / MANIFESTO

Ethics and artistic responsibility

Human artists learn from predecessors, but machine training and human learning are not identical. Training-data consent, copyright, and compensation remain important questions. Generative systems can reproduce protected expression even without a deliberate copying request. Artistic merit, training legality, and output infringement must be evaluated separately.

09 / AESTHETICS

Practice and possibilities

The accompanying paper develops eight principles of artistic practice, including creative conception, human direction, intentional revision, and transparency. A single discussion of creative labor explains the artist’s responsibility to provide a brief statement of purpose, rationale, and workflow. It also explores the medium’s aesthetic possibilities and its potential to widen access to creation. By enabling works that would otherwise never be made, synthetic media can increase the net total and range of art and creative projects, with potentially profound benefits for individual expression, community storytelling, and shared creative participation. Participatory Generativity is a distinctive optional capability: an artist can create an interactive framework that adapts to audience choices without making interactivity a requirement of SMA.

10 / MANIFESTO

About this proposal

Proposed by Justin Plevinski. Overview version 1.1, updated October 21, 2026. This public overview presents a framework for Synthetic Media Art and Creation, including SMC as a category of substantially human-directed creation and SMA as a more rigorously directed artistic practice. It acknowledges earlier practice associated with Bill Posters and documented use of “synthetic media art” by Annka Kultys Gallery in 2021, without claiming to identify the term’s originator. Plevinski developed the initial formulation independently and now situates it within prior scholarship, including Epstein and colleagues’ work on generative AI as an artistic medium and Memo Akten’s research on meaningful human control. The contribution proposed here is a particular synthesis and five-dimensional account of substantial human direction, rather than priority over the terminology or the broader ideas. The full academic argument is available on the Academic Argument page; its references are provided for further examination.
